Meal Plan

Five eating slots a day. Each slot below is a menu — pick one option. They're built to land you near ~2,400 kcal / ~190–200g protein on training days. All lacto-vegetarian, cuisines mixed. Macros are approximate; consistency beats precision.

Daily target: ~2,400 kcal · ~190g protein · ~250g carbs · ~70g fat (training day).  |  Rest day: drop ~200 kcal, mostly from the carb portions (smaller rice/oats/bread).  |  P = protein. Add creatine 3–5g daily (any time) and a daily multivitamin + B12 (sensible for vegetarians).
Half your plateFor your BP

Vegetables — make them half the plate

Fair callout: the first draft chased the protein number and treated veg as a garnish. For your goals they deserve top billing — aim for 5–7 fist-sized servings a day and fill ~half your lunch and dinner plate with them.

Why it matters for you: vegetables are the backbone of the DASH pattern that lowers blood pressure — loading you with potassium, nitrates and fibre that directly counter sodium. And in a deficit they're your best friend: big volume + fibre for almost no calories, so you stay full while losing fat.

Best potassium picks for BP: spinach, beet greens, Swiss chard, sweet potato, beans, tomato, mushrooms, broccoli, beetroot.

5 AMPre- & post-workout fuelaround the lift

On waking (~4:30): 400–600 ml water + black coffee or pre-workout + your creatine. That morning coffee doubles as your training boost and clears well before an early bedtime.

Heavy days (Legs / Push / Pull) — small fast snack 15–30 min before: a banana, a few dates, half a protein shake, or toast with honey. Not breakfast — just enough to hold intensity on the big lifts.

Easy days (Zone 2 / mobility): fine fully fasted (water + coffee) — a good spot to nudge fat oxidation.

Right after (~6:00–6:30): the real meal — that's Meal 1 below (protein + carbs) to start recovery. Don't skip it.

Warm-up & Stretch

At 5 AM your body is cold, stiff and straight out of bed — exactly when joints get tweaked. A proper dynamic warm-up here is the difference between full development and a layoff. The evidence-based rule: dynamic movement before, static holds after.

Why this order: static stretching before lifting can cut power 5–10% and doesn't reduce injury risk; dynamic warm-ups raise muscle temperature, mobility and performance and do lower injury risk. Long holds belong after, when muscles are warm.
Every session · dynamic warm-up (8–10 min)

1. Raise the temperature (5 min). Easy bike, row or incline walk until lightly sweating. Give it the full 5 min at 5 AM.

2. Dynamic mobility — 2 rounds of the day's list below.

3. Ramp-up sets. On the first big lift, 2–3 progressively heavier sets (~50/70/85%) before your first working set.

Leg days
  • Leg swings — front/back ×10 each leg ▶
  • Lateral leg swings ×10 each leg ▶
  • Bodyweight squats ×15 (deeper each rep) ▶
  • Walking lunge with torso twist ×8/leg ▶
  • 90/90 hip rotations ×8/side ▶
  • Ankle rocks, knee over toe ×10/side ▶
  • Glute bridges ×15 ▶
Push / Pull days
  • Arm circles, small→large ×15 each way ▶
  • Band pull-aparts ×20 ▶
  • Band shoulder dislocates ×10 (wide, gentle) ▶
  • Scapular push-ups ×10 ▶
  • Cat–cow ×8 ▶
  • Thoracic rotations ×8/side ▶
  • Light external rotations ×15 ▶
After the session · static stretch (5–8 min, hold 30 s each)
Lower
  • Standing / seated hamstring stretch ▶
  • Standing quad stretch ▶
  • Kneeling hip-flexor lunge stretch ▶
  • Calf stretch — straight + bent knee ▶
  • Figure-4 / pigeon glute stretch ▶
  • Seated adductor (butterfly) stretch ▶
Upper
  • Doorway pec stretch ▶
  • Cross-body shoulder stretch ▶
  • Overhead triceps stretch ▶
  • Lat stretch (hang or side reach) ▶
  • Child's pose ▶
  • Thread-the-needle (thoracic) ▶
Daily · your shoulder-tightness fix

5 min most days (great for desk breaks too). Staying ahead of the tightness keeps your pressing healthy and lets the upper body grow without flare-ups.

  • Band shoulder dislocates ×15 — slow, no pain ▶
  • Wall slides ×10 ▶
  • Thoracic extension over foam roller ×10 ▶
  • Doorway pec stretch — 2 × 30 s/side ▶
  • Cross-body + sleeper stretch (gentle) ▶

Behm et al. systematic review & Mayo Clinic: dynamic warm-ups are the preferred pre-activity strategy; static stretching is best post-workout (pre-lift static >60 s blunts strength — keep any under ~45 s). Sharp/pinching shoulder pain = stop and see a physio; never push through it.

Workouts

Every session, move by move. AM = your main lift; PM = optional accessory (skip on meeting / bad-sleep days). Built for an experienced lifter — these are working prescriptions, not tutorials.

Sets×Reps = working sets after warm-ups.  RIR = reps in reserve (how many you stop short of failure).  Rest shown per exercise.
Before set 1 of any compound: 2–3 ramp-up sets (~50%, 70%, 85% of working load).  General warm-up: 5 min easy cardio + dynamic mobility for the day's muscles.
Breathing (BP-critical): exhale on the hard part, never hold your breath / brace-and-strain (no Valsalva). Stay 1–2 RIR; no all-out 1RM grinds.
Start every session in the Warm-up & Stretch tab. At 5 AM a 5-min temperature raise + dynamic mobility is non-negotiable — it's how you train hard and stay injury-free. Static stretching goes after, never before.

Fitting the PM work as an early bird: with a ~9 PM bedtime, either (a) tack the 20–25 min accessory block onto the end of your morning session, or (b) do it early evening (~5:30–6 PM) — just keep heavy work out of the last 3 h before bed. On meeting days, drop it.

Progression & recovery rules
  • Double progression: stay at the same load until you hit the top of the rep range on all working sets, then add the smallest increment (2.5–5 lb upper, 5–10 lb lower) and drop back to the bottom of the range.
  • Log every main lift (Log tab) so you can actually see the progression instead of guessing.
  • Deload weeks 4 and 9: cut working sets ~40%, keep technique crisp. This is when your joints and your blood pressure recover — don't skip it.
  • Auto-regulate: on a 5-hour-sleep day, drop the PM session and pull the AM back to the easier end of the rep ranges. Pushing through poor recovery at 40 is how injuries and BP spikes happen.
  • Upper/biceps growth check: if arms aren't progressing by week 5, the fix is usually more sleep and protein, not more sets — recovery is the ceiling here.

Cardio & Fat Loss

What Zone 2 actually is, and an honest ranking of what burns fat — so your energy goes to the levers that matter, not the ones that just feel busy.

Zone 2, explained

Zone 2 is steady cardio at ~60–70% of max heart rate — the highest intensity where your body still burns fat as its main fuel and stays below the lactate threshold. No gadget needed: use the talk test — breathing harder but able to hold a full conversation. Gasping means you've drifted too high.

Why it's in your plan: it maximizes fat oxidation, builds your aerobic base (more mitochondria + capillaries), lowers resting blood pressure, and — key for you — has a low recovery cost, so it won't eat your leg recovery the way hard running would. Incline walk, bike, row, or easy jog all count.

Cleveland Clinic, HPRC (US DoD) & aerobic-exercise meta-analyses: moderate-intensity aerobic work reliably lowers systolic/diastolic BP and improves fat oxidation and metabolic flexibility.

What actually burns fat — in order
1

The calorie deficit (diet)

The engine. Your ~350 kcal/day deficit is what creates fat loss — no cardio out-runs the fork. Already built into your meal targets.

2

Protein — 190 g

Ensures what you lose is fat, not hard-won muscle, and keeps you full. The most important macro in a cut.

3

Daily steps / NEAT — 8,000–10,000

The most underrated lever, and the one a desk job kills. All-day movement often out-burns the workout. Walk after meals (also great for BP & blood sugar) and move hourly.

4

Zone 2 cardio

Adds to the deficit, burns fat as fuel, recoverable alongside heavy legs. Your 2–3 weekly sessions.

5

Sleep — 7.5 h

Short sleep spikes hunger hormones and cortisol and makes a deficit miserable. The early bedtime isn't optional if fat loss is the goal.

6

Strength training

Builds/keeps muscle → higher resting metabolism and the muscular look once the fat's off.

7

Intervals / HIIT (Saturday)

Time-efficient with an afterburn (EPOC) — but research shows total fat loss is similar to Zone 2. Use it as a weekly sprinkle, not the main course.

On fasted 5 AM training: it can modestly raise fat burning during the session, but the total daily deficit is what determines fat loss — so don't treat fasted cardio as magic. Fuel your heavy lifts (Meal Plan → pre-workout card); save fully-fasted for easy Zone 2 or walks.

Habits & Sleep

5–6 h is your biggest brake — on recovery, on arm/leg growth, on appetite, on blood pressure. Getting to 7.5 h will lift every goal at once.

Your new clock · 5 AM gym ⇒ 9 PM bed
  • The non-negotiable trade: a 5 AM gym means a ~4:30 wake, and 7.5 h of sleep then requires lights-out by ~9:00 PM. Training early without moving bedtime earlier just deepens the under-recovery you're in now.
  • Anchor the wake time — same time every day, weekends included. Your 5 AM alarm already does this.
  • Morning daylight within ~30 min of waking — your AM workout handles this for free.
  • Front-load caffeine, cut off by ~1 PM. Your 5 AM pre-workout coffee is perfectly timed and clears long before bed; the afternoon chai/coffee is what wrecks the 5–6 h sleep. The early schedule makes caffeine easy.
  • Dim the last 2 hours — overhead lights off, screens on night mode after ~8:30.
  • Cool, dark, quiet room — 16–19°C, blackout/mask.
  • Dinner by ~7 PM, wind-down by ~8:30. Late food and screens push sleep onset past 9 — the whole day shifts earlier now.
  • Evening-meeting wind-down: 20–30 min decompress (yoga/stretch/hot shower, no email) so a late meeting doesn't eat your sleep.
  • No heavy lifting in the 3 h before bed — mobility/yoga only if you train late.

Better sleep lowers blood pressure and blunts the appetite/cortisol effects that make a deficit harder — it compounds with training and nutrition rather than competing.
